\title{Open System-One Decision Models:\\
Frozen Diffusion-LM Backbones and Specialist Decision Heads\\
for Calibrated Scientific Decisions}
\author{Anonymous}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}
\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Structured decisions---selecting among options, verifying whether evidence
supports a claim, and grading response quality on ordinal scales---are the
atomic operations of automated scientific pipelines. Proprietary
``System-One'' models deliver such decisions as typed API calls without
generation, but are closed. We present an open, trainable counterpart: a
frozen masked-diffusion language model backbone (LLaDA-8B) with small
specialist decision heads producing probabilities for three decision types
(choice, noul, score). We contribute three findings. (1)~A deterministic
canonical ordering gives a permutation-invariant input sequence for
options with distinct token representations, rather than relying on
train-time order augmentation. (2)~Multi-layer attention pooling over
option spans outperforms last-layer mean pooling for choice ($+7$ points),
and the combined specialist, canonical, and ordinal recipe improves graded
scoring ($+9$ points).
(3)~Scaling training data $30\times$ on an out-of-domain corpus improves
in-distribution accuracy to $0.96$ but does not transfer ($-13$ points on
the target domain), suggesting that volume alone is insufficient. On
public benchmarks, \ours{} reaches 0.8500 on SciFact claim verification
and transfers zero-shot to general classification (0.9300 SST-2, 0.8537
AG News); knowledge-intensive GPQA remains difficult (0.3125 on main).
Candidates using LoRA domain adaptation score higher on elite choice
but lower on GPQA and SciFact. Their head-training schedules also differ,
so this is a candidate comparison, not an isolated causal test of DAPT.
The release retains the frozen-backbone heads.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Automated scientific pipelines---agentic literature review, claim
verification, data extraction, routing to slower reasoning
components---are built out of small structured judgments: \emph{which}
of these options answers the question, \emph{does} the evidence support
the claim, \emph{how} good is this candidate answer. Today these
judgments are typically extracted from generative LLMs by prompting for
JSON and parsing the output, a pipeline that is slow, brittle under
format drift, and poorly calibrated.

A recent class of models removes generation entirely: given an
unstructured state and a set of typed questions, a \emph{System-One}
model returns typed values---a choice among options, a yes/no
probability, an ordinal score---each with a calibrated probability, in a
single forward pass. The flagship proprietary model of this class is
Jev (TypeSafe AI, 2026); open reimplementations appeared within days of
its release (kev, openjev, laya, SemIf), all built on autoregressive or
encoder backbones.

We ask what an open System-One model needs to be good at, and build one
on the other major family of language models: a \emph{masked-diffusion}
backbone (LLaDA-8B), whose bidirectional attention is a natural fit for
decision readouts that compare each option against the full context.
To preserve the pretrained weights and limit trainable parameters, we
train specialist heads per decision type on frozen multi-layer hidden
states. Our contributions:

\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Permutation invariance by canonical ordering.}
Sorting options deterministically by content makes the presentation
order a function of the option set. For distinct token representations,
permutations produce the same input sequence; train-time order averaging
alone does not impose this constraint.
\item \textbf{Specialist heads per decision type.} Multi-layer
attention pooling over option spans improves choice ($+7$ points over
mean pooling) while hurting binary verification---different decision
types want different readouts. The combined specialist, canonical, and
ordinal recipe improves graded scoring ($+9$ points).
\item \textbf{Data volume and adaptation are not sufficient.} A
$30\times$ out-of-domain data increase fails to transfer ($-13$ points).
We also compare release candidates with and without LoRA domain
adaptation. The adapted candidates improve some elite metrics but lose
accuracy on GPQA and SciFact. We retain the frozen-backbone candidate;
matching the head optimization schedules is necessary before drawing
causal conclusions about adaptation.
\end{itemize}

We evaluate on the elite scientific battery, GPQA, SciFact, and four
classification datasets. Metrics cover accuracy, calibration, permutation
consistency, and retrospective automation at 5\% error.

\section{Setup}
\paragraph{Decision types.}
We consider three typed decisions over a state (context) and a question:
\textbf{choice} selects one of $K$ answer options; \textbf{noul} decides
whether evidence supports a claim ($K{=}2$); \textbf{score} assigns an
ordinal grade $0 < 1 < 2$ (unrelated / related-but-wrong / correct).

\paragraph{Model.}
The released span-pooling heads encode context, question, and options as
one observed token sequence, without inserting mask markers or generating
text. A frozen masked-diffusion backbone (LLaDA-8B-Instruct) produces token
representations. A mean-pooled MLP reads final-layer option spans for
noul and score; \emph{AttnPool} learns a query for each of layers
$\{8,16,24,32\}$ and concatenates the pooled features for choice. The
released choice head has 67{,}166{,}209 parameters; each MLP has
16{,}793{,}601. Only these heads are trained during decision training.

\paragraph{Canonical ordering.}
Options are sorted by token-id content before encoding, and gold indices
remapped. For options that remain distinct after tokenization and
truncation, any input permutation collapses to the same sequence.
Identical or truncated-to-identical options require a separate tie policy
if their external identifiers must remain distinguishable.

\paragraph{Ordinal auxiliary loss.}
For score we add a CORAL-style term
$\sum_{j=1}^{K-1} \mathrm{BCE}\big(P(y \ge j),\,\mathbf{1}[g \ge j]\big)$
on cumulative softmax mass, respecting the grade order without changing the
$K$-way decode.

\paragraph{Metrics.}
We report accuracy, expected calibration error (ECE), option-order flip
rate (a change in the selected option's original identity after
permutation), and \emph{automation@5\%err}: the largest confidence-ranked
prefix with realized error $\le 5\%$. Automation is retrospective on
labeled evaluation data, not a guarantee of deployment error. Historical
non-canonical flip figures compared output positions without remapping
option identities; they are excluded here pending re-evaluation. The
release fixes that metric and includes regression tests.

\section{Experiments}
\subsection{Scientific decision battery}
The \emph{elite} battery is built from passage-grounded scientific QA
typed by reasoning kind (factual, causal, multihop, negation,
numerical, definitional); each passage generates a choice item ($K{=}4$,
one gold + three distractors), a noul item (supported vs.\ unsupported
claim), and a score item (correct / related-but-wrong / unrelated
answer). Splits are passage-disjoint to prevent leakage
(2{,}240/6{,}720/6{,}720 train rows per type). The \emph{v2} battery
scales the same construction to 25{,}000 passages from three raw QA
corpora (physics/polymer LaTeX full texts, astronomy, and a derived
set) with groundedness filters---content-word recall $\ge 0.5$ and
numeric binding---yielding $\sim$470{,}000 training decisions; we use
it to probe the data-scaling question.

\subsection{Public benchmarks}
GPQA main (448 graduate-level questions) and diamond subset (198) as
choice; SciFact dev (340 claim--abstract pairs) as noul and 3-way score;
plus general classification (SST-2, AG News, Enron spam, Banking77) to
probe out-of-domain transfer. Benchmark examples are not used for gradient
updates or temperature fitting; temperatures are fitted on the battery
dev split. Benchmark outcomes did inform the choice of release candidate.

\subsection{Candidate protocols and comparison limits}
The frozen release heads (\texttt{c\_*}) use learning rate $3\times10^{-4}$,
accumulation 1, warmup 200, and 2{,}000/3{,}000/3{,}000 example steps for
choice/noul/score. Heads for the adapted candidates (\texttt{dag\_*},
\texttt{da\_*}) and the small-backbone control (\texttt{eb\_*}) instead use
learning rate $10^{-3}$, accumulation 8, warmup 100, and 3{,}000 steps per
type. All use seed 7331, the same elite training files, the same head
architecture per type, and an ordinal auxiliary weight of 1 for score.
Thus these are candidate comparisons, not a controlled DAPT-only ablation.

DAPT uses LoRA rank 16, alpha 32, dropout 0.05, and the masked-token
objective implemented in \texttt{reverse\_jev.dapt}. With batch size 16
and sequence cap 1{,}024, the 2{,}000/5{,}000-step adapters correspond to
at most 32.8/81.9 million input token slots, not measured counts of unique
non-padding tokens. The 5{,}000-step run warm-starts from the
2{,}000-step adapter with a fresh optimizer and a schedule recomputed for
the shorter total budget; optimizer and RNG state were not restored.
The 1.4B \texttt{bw1\_sr} control is checkpoint-g20 from a 20-step run,
with a 768-token window versus 2{,}048 for LLaDA. It is an early-training
control, not evidence about the capacity of a fully trained 1.4B model.

Calibration (dev-fitted temperature, frozen at evaluation) is strong
in-distribution: elite ECE is 0.0167/0.0189/0.0118 for choice/noul/score

matched design therefore rejects a uniform verdict in either direction:
adaptation effects are task-dependent, not monotone.

\section{Discussion}

\paragraph{What a decision layer buys.}
A decision layer exposes probabilities without text generation or output
parsing. On the elite battery, the frozen heads have ECE 0.0253--0.1221
and choice automation@5\% of 0.8070. This retrospective operating curve
can inform routing to a slower component, but a deployment threshold
must be validated independently under its own data distribution.

\paragraph{Backbone and head must be evaluated together.}
Readout architecture changes accuracy even with a fixed backbone, while
candidate performance also varies with the backbone, context window,
and training schedule. The early bw1\_sr checkpoint cannot establish a
size-dependent knowledge ceiling. Likewise, proximity to a published
GPQA score under another inference protocol would not prove that our
head extracts all available knowledge.

\paragraph{Candidate selection is not a causal adaptation study.}
Table~\ref{tab:candidates} supports retaining the frozen heads for this
release: the adapted candidates do not satisfy the transfer gate despite
some improved elite metrics. It does not establish that domain adaptation
necessarily sacrifices generalization, nor that a domain-native backbone
would avoid that trade-off. The matched comparison in
Table~\ref{tab:matched} holds head optimization fixed on
\texttt{systemone-v2} and shows the causal effect is task-dependent:
adaptation transfers to external verification while degrading ordinal
scoring. A fuller study should still repeat seeds, restore optimizer
state across interruptions, and reserve untouched evaluation data after
selection.

\paragraph{Relation to the ecosystem.}
Concurrent System-One implementations motivate typed decision interfaces.
Our implementation explores a masked-diffusion backbone and deterministic
option ordering. A numerical ranking against other systems would require
matched dataset conversions, splits, and calibration protocols; the
empirical tables here report only our own evaluated candidates.

\section{Limitations}
Backbone pretraining may include benchmark-adjacent text. Heads are trained
on one decision distribution and on at most four options; Banking77
performance is poor. Score remains weaker than choice on the elite
battery. Results are single runs, without multi-seed uncertainty
estimates. Candidate comparisons differ in optimization, and the small
backbone is only an early-training control. External benchmarks informed
release selection, so they are not an untouched final test set. We report
archived evaluation outputs and their hashes, not a new evaluation of the
full backbone during release packaging.
